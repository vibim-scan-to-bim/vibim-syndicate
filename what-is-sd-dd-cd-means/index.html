SD, DD and CD: Technical Reference for the Three Design Phases

Scope

This page defines the three design phases standardized by the American Institute of Architects (AIA). It lists the purpose, deliverables, level of detail and approval gate for each phase, then shows how a single BIM model can carry the project through all three.

Definitions

Schematic Design (SD) is the first phase. The architect converts the client's program and goals into a high-level concept covering form, scale, massing and the relationship between spaces.

Design Development (DD) is the second phase. The approved concept is refined with technical detail: structural systems, materials, and the layout of mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) systems are decided.

Construction Documents (CD) is the final phase. The design is translated into drawings and specifications used for contractor bidding, permit applications and construction.

Comparison

Attribute SD DD CD
Purpose Establish concept, scale, spatial relationships Refine design, make technical decisions, coordinate systems Issue complete instructions for bidding, permitting, building
Level of detail Low, conceptual Medium, refined High, technical
Client approval Overall concept and direction Specific materials, systems, layouts Full drawing and specification set
Coordination Minimal between disciplines Intensive between architecture, structure and MEP Final, across all disciplines
Typical duration Shortest Longer than SD Often the longest

Deliverables by phase

  1. SD: schematic site plan, floor plans, exterior elevations, key building sections, conceptual visualizations, narrative description.
  2. DD: fully dimensioned drawings, structural design, MEP layout, outline specifications, updated cost estimate.
  3. CD: final architectural drawings, final engineering drawings, technical specifications manual, bidding and contract documents.

Model-based workflow

A BIM workflow keeps one model that gains detail as the phases advance.

  • SD: for existing buildings, reality-capture data is converted into a measured model so massing starts from real conditions.
  • DD: architecture, structure and MEP are modeled in one environment, which allows early clash detection.
  • CD: a higher level of development lets drawings, sections and schedules be extracted from the model instead of drawn by hand.

Known constraints

  • The phases are sequential. A late change to the approved SD concept forces rework in DD and CD.
  • CD is treated as frozen design. Corrections after issue tend to become change orders.
  • Level of detail should be set per phase and per component before modeling starts, so the model matches the expected output.
