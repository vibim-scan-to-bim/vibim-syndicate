Outsourcing Revit Modeling: Selection Criteria, Evidence and Process Reference

This page is a compact reference for teams evaluating an external Revit BIM modeling provider. It lists what to ask, what evidence to request and in what order to run the process.

Why selection matters

The choice of provider affects timelines, budget, deliverable quality, data security and reputation. Typical failure modes are missed deadlines, models that cannot be used for clash detection or quantity takeoffs, and exposure of shared project data.

Evaluation criteria

Criterion What to ask Evidence to request
Technical expertise Which project types and scales have they delivered? Portfolio and case studies close to your scope
LOD capability Can they work at LOD 200, 350 and 400? Sample models at each level
Modeling competency How do they respond to a sample scope? Written questions about scope, risks and deliverables
Infrastructure Which workstations and software versions are in use? Hardware configuration, current Revit and Navisworks versions
Industry and region Do they know local standards? Experience with the US National BIM Standard or the ISO 19650 series
Quality assurance How are models reviewed? QA/QC procedure covering clashes, data integrity and standards
Communication Who is the contact and how often are updates sent? Named project manager, update schedule, time zone overlap
Pricing and contract What is included in the quote? Itemized costs, clauses for scope, IP and data ownership
Data security How is project data protected? NDA, secure transfer, access controls, daily backups
Reputation What do past clients say? References and independent online reviews

Definitions

  • LOD (Level of Development): the level of detail and data reliability a model element must reach at a given project stage.
  • Pilot project: a small, paid, clearly bounded piece of real work used to test a provider before a long-term contract.
  • QA/QC: the review and validation steps applied to a model before delivery.
  • NDA: a legally binding agreement not to disclose project information.

Process

  1. Define scope: LOD, deliverables, timeline and standards.
  2. Build a shortlist and vet it against the table above.
  3. Request proposals and compare understanding of the scope, workflow and team, not only price.
  4. Hold a video interview with the person who will be your point of contact.
  5. Sign a contract and agree a communication plan.
  6. Manage delivery with regular check-ins and feedback on each deliverable.

Notes

If the project starts from laser scans, check the provider's ability to convert point clouds to Revit models and ask how scan-based models are verified against the captured data. A free or paid demo is the most direct way to see this before committing.
