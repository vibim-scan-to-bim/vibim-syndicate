BIM Standards Reference: Frameworks, Documents and Scope

A quick technical reference to the main BIM standards and what each one governs.

Core definition

BIM standards are guidelines, protocols and best practices that define how BIM is implemented and managed across a project lifecycle. They set a structured way to produce, exchange and manage digital information so data stays consistent, accurate and interoperable.

International framework

Document Scope
ISO 19650-1 Concepts and principles, including the Common Data Environment (CDE) and the roles of the parties
ISO 19650-2 Delivery phase of assets: information exchange from project start to handover
ISO 19650-3 Operational phase of assets
ISO 19650-4 Information exchange between parties
ISO 19650-5 Security-minded approach to information management

ISO 19650 is a flexible framework. It does not prescribe software or a fixed workflow.

United Kingdom lineage

Document Role
BS 1192:2007 Collaborative production of information, with a naming convention
PAS 1192-2:2013 Capital and delivery phase; introduced BIM maturity Levels 0 to 3
PAS 1192-3:2014 Operational phase of assets
BS 1192-4:2014 COBie methodology for facility management information
PAS 1192-5:2015 Security-minded approach

United States documents

Document Role
NBIMS-US Consensus-based standard from NIBS covering the building lifecycle
BIMForum LOD specification Reliability and content of model elements, LOD 100 to LOD 500
USIBD LOA specification Accuracy of positions and geometry captured from reality capture
GSA BIM standards Requirements for projects of a major public building owner
AIA documents and AGC BIM Addendum Contract language for roles, responsibilities and legal implications

Standard versus protocol

A standard is the general rulebook. A protocol is a project-specific document with granular procedures such as file naming, coordination routines and data exchange formats. In short, the standard gives the "what" and the protocol gives the "how".

Open exchange

Industry Foundation Classes (IFC), maintained by buildingSMART International, is the neutral open format that lets different BIM applications exchange data without loss.

Practical steps

  1. Read the owner's information requirements.
  2. Confirm the CDE structure and naming rules.
  3. Check LOD (and LOA for existing buildings) per project stage.
  4. Export IFC and open it in a second application.
  5. Record results in a log.
