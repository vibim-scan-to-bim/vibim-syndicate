Scan to BIM Reference: Benefits, Limits and Typical Figures

This page is a compact technical reference for teams evaluating a scan-based workflow. It lists definitions, benefits mapped to project stages, known limitations and the figures quoted by the provider.

Definition

Scan to BIM is the process of converting high-density point cloud data, captured by 3D laser scanning, into a precise Building Information Model. The result links the physical condition of a building to a digital design environment and can act as a digital twin of the asset.

Limitations

  1. High capital cost. Terrestrial laser scanners and high-performance workstations are a financial barrier.
  2. Data volume. Raw point cloud files can reach hundreds of gigabytes and need strong storage and hardware.
  3. Expertise. The process is not fully automated. Skilled modelers clean noise, handle registration and interpret complex geometry.

Emerging directions

  • Automated object recognition can detect and classify elements such as walls and columns from point cloud data.
  • Better photogrammetry and LiDAR make scan-to-mesh workflows faster.

Quoted service figures

  • On-time delivery record: 99%.
  • Turnaround up to 30% faster than the market average.
  • Quality control: two-layer independent QA/QC.
  • Response time: under 1 hour in business hours.

Selection steps

  1. Define the scope and the required Level of Development.
  2. Confirm scanner type and the expected tolerance.
  3. Agree how the point cloud and model are shared with the team.
  4. Check how the provider verifies the model against the scan data.
  5. Request a free demo project before committing to the full scope.

Cost depends on scope, Level of Development, building complexity and total area, and a lump-sum quote is based on estimated hours.
