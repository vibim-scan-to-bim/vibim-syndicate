Scan to BIM Terms: Technical Glossary and Reference Tables

This page is a compact reference for the vocabulary used when a laser scan becomes a Building Information Model. Terms are grouped by the stage of the workflow where they first matter, so a team can look up a word at the moment it appears in a brief, a scope or a QA report.

Definition

Scan to BIM is the process of capturing the as-built conditions of a physical space with 3D laser scanners and converting that data into a BIM. The vocabulary below describes what is captured, how it is aligned, how it is modeled and how the result is checked.

Capture terms

Term Meaning
Reality capture Generating digital representations of physical environments through laser scanning or photogrammetry
Point cloud Data points in a 3D coordinate system; each point can carry attributes such as color or intensity
3D laser scanning Laser pulses measure the time of reflection to calculate distance; several scan positions avoid gaps and shadows
LiDAR Distance measurement by emitted laser pulses; the core technology inside most terrestrial laser scanners
Photogrammetry 3D models from overlapping photographs; often cheaper and colorful, with lower geometric accuracy

Alignment terms

Term Meaning
Scan registration Aligning several scans into one coordinate system through reference points shared between them
Mis-alignment Registration error that shows up as ghosting or distortion; also the gap between model and point cloud beyond tolerance
Coordinate The X, Y, Z reference that places points and objects in the model or on site
Project Base Point The reference point of the Revit project's internal coordinate system
Survey Point The real-world location, used for shared coordinates
Origin to Origin Placement that matches the internal (0,0,0) of a linked file to the Revit model origin

Modeling and specification terms

Term Meaning
As-built model A BIM that reflects the building as constructed, including deviations from the design plans
Parametric modeling Geometry driven by parameters and constraints, so the model updates when values change
LOD Level of Development: completeness and reliability of an element, from 100 to 500
LOA Level of Accuracy: acceptable deviation between model and reality; LOA 10 allows 5 to 15 cm, LOA 50 allows 0 to 1 mm
LOI Level of Information: the non-graphical data on an element, such as material, manufacturer or maintenance schedule
Tolerance The permissible variation; for example +/- 5 mm between a modeled surface and the scanned points
Setup file The record of scanner positions and orientations, or the adjusted starting Revit file with coordinates, levels and families

Downstream terms

Term Meaning
Clash detection Software finds interference between elements, such as a new duct crossing an existing beam
Digital twin A virtual replica that reflects its physical counterpart; an as-built model is often its foundation
TrueView Leica Geosystems web viewer that overlays panoramic photos on scan data for browser-based review

Checking order

  1. Register the scans and confirm the alignment.
  2. Fix the coordinate system and the setup file.
  3. Agree LOD, LOA, LOI and tolerance in writing.
  4. Model the elements and compare the model with the point cloud against the tolerance.
  5. Run clash detection against proposed designs.

Each step depends on the one before it: a modeling tolerance means little when registration already drifted.
