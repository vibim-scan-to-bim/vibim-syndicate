Shop Drawings vs As-Built Drawings: Technical Reference

This reference summarizes how shop drawings and as-built drawings differ in purpose, timing and use, so teams can pick the right document for each task.

Definitions

Shop drawings are detailed illustrations prepared by contractors, subcontractors, suppliers or manufacturers. They describe how specific components are manufactured, assembled and installed, including dimensions, material specifications, assembly instructions and connection details.

As-built drawings, also called record or redline drawings, are the documented state of a project as it was actually constructed. They start from the design drawings and are updated during construction to reflect on-site changes.

Comparison

Attribute Shop drawings As-built drawings
Purpose Guide fabrication and installation of components Document the final constructed state
Stage Before construction begins During and after construction
Scope One component or system, such as HVAC or steel beams The entire building
Audience Fabricators, installers, project team for approval Owners, facility managers, renovation teams
Creator Contractor, subcontractor, supplier or fabricator General contractor, from field markups
Deviations Propose how to meet design intent; do not record deviations Record deviations made during construction
Legal role Part of the submittal process before work begins Permanent record of the completed work

What an as-built set records

  • Location changes, such as the final position of walls, windows or doors
  • Material changes, meaning substitutions for specified materials
  • Dimensional changes to rooms or components
  • Installation changes, such as rerouted HVAC, electrical or plumbing runs

Typical workflow

  1. The architect and engineer issue the design documents.
  2. A subcontractor prepares shop drawings for a system, for example ductwork.
  3. The architect and engineer review and approve them.
  4. The subcontractor fabricates and installs from the approved set.
  5. Site changes, such as a reroute around an unexpected beam, are marked on the master drawings.
  6. The markups are used to produce the final as-built drawings.

Related terms

Record drawings are normally prepared by the architect or engineer, who take the contractor's as-built information and issue a clean, official set to the owner. The two terms are often used interchangeably, although the preparer differs.

Shop drawings and as-builts are not interchangeable. One is a proactive pre-construction document and the other a reactive post-construction record, and substituting one for the other leads to errors in fabrication or in facility management.
