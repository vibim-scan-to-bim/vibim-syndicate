3D Laser Scanning: Technical Reference for Survey and BIM Teams

This page is a compact reference for the main terms, scanner types, quality factors and handover formats used when laser scan data feeds a BIM workflow. It is written for quick lookup before a capture is commissioned.

Definition

3D laser scanning is a non-contact measurement method. A laser measures the distance to every surface it can see and records each return as an X, Y, Z coordinate. The output of a scan session is a point cloud. It is not a drawing and not a model, so as-built drawings and Revit models are produced from the cloud in a separate step.

Workflow stages

Stage Where What happens
Set-up Field The scanner is placed with clear line of sight to the surfaces that matter.
Capture Field The head sweeps the beam and logs returns; the scanner moves to several positions.
Registration Office Separate scans are aligned into one coordinate system using the overlap between positions.
Processing Office Noise from moving people, reflective glass and dust is filtered out.

Scanner technologies

Technology Working range (datasheet examples) Typical use
Time-of-flight 0.3 m to 130 m (Leica RTC360); up to 1,000 m (Leica ScanStation P50) Open sites, exteriors, infrastructure
Phase-shift 0.3 m to 365 m (Z+F IMAGER 5016) Interiors, plant rooms, dense MEP
Triangulation 0.5 m to 2 m Individual objects and components

Range belongs to the scanner model, not to the ranging method. A phase-shift unit can out-reach a time-of-flight unit, so specifying by technology alone can mislead.

Quality factors that limit LOD

  1. Point density sets the smallest feature that survives in the data.
  2. Coverage determines whether hidden elements such as risers exist at all.
  3. Registration accuracy decides whether geometry stays aligned across a whole building.

Mobile or handheld capture has lower density and supports roughly LOD 300 across a model. A registered terrestrial scan supports higher LOD and tighter tolerance.

Handover formats

  • Point clouds: RCP and RCS preferred for the Autodesk toolchain; E57 as the vendor-neutral option, usually converted first.
  • Transfer: RCS files at 5 GB or under, or one zip per transfer.
  • Models: RVT, IFC with the schema version stated, DWG for 2D sheets.

Common reasons a cloud is unusable

  • Geometry was never captured because of blocked line of sight.
  • Positions did not register cleanly, so one wall appears twice.
  • Data arrived incomplete and elements cannot be classified.
  • The cloud deviates from the drawings and the difference is unresolved.
