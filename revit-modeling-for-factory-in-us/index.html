Definitions

  • Point cloud: a set of measured 3D points captured from the existing building, used as the reference for modeling.
  • LOD 300: a level of development where elements have accurate geometry, size and location.
  • MEP: mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems.
  • As-built model: a model that records the existing condition of a facility, not its design intent.

Disciplines modeled

  1. Architecture
  2. Structure
  3. Topography
  4. MEP, including equipment, water pipes, cable trays and electrical conduits

Constraints in the data

  • Density: machine rooms, technical areas and transformer stations contained very high concentrations of equipment, pipes and cables.
  • Occlusion and noise: overlapping objects hid surfaces in the scan.
  • Connection accuracy: pipe fittings, valve connections, electrical terminations and control links had to be represented exactly.

Procedure

  1. Divide the plant into smaller zones and apply quality control per zone.
  2. Model large, well-defined MEP elements first, such as main pipelines and primary equipment.
  3. Add smaller components progressively around that backbone.
  4. Use sectioning, layering and element isolation to reach hidden objects.
  5. Switch the point cloud to RGB display and raise contrast to separate pipes, cables and equipment from clutter.
  6. Identify elements from characteristic shapes (circular, square, rectangular) where data is partial.
  7. Model every flange, valve and junction with the highest accuracy.

Intended uses of the model

  • Baseline for renovation and retrofit planning.
  • Coordination between architectural, structural and MEP teams.
  • Maintenance planning and asset records.
  • Safety review of gas, chemical and high-power electrical routes.

Result

The model gives the client a centralized digital database of MEP components. Technicians can locate equipment and connection points faster, which helps reduce downtime during troubleshooting and repair.
