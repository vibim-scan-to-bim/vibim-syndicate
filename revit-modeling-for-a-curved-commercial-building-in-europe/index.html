Project 302.EU Technical Reference: Curved Walls and Double-Skin Glass in a Scan to BIM Model

This page is a technical reference for how a curved commercial building in the European Union was modeled in Revit from a laser scan point cloud. It records the project parameters, the three geometry problems and the method used for each.

Definitions

  • As-built BIM model: a Revit model that records a building as it stands, rather than as originally designed.
  • LOD 300: a level of detail where elements are modeled with accurate geometry, size and position.
  • Point cloud: a set of 3D points captured by laser scanning.
  • Heat map: a color comparison of a model against the point cloud, green for a match and red for drift.
  • Workset: a Revit mechanism for dividing a model so parts can be opened or closed independently.

Problem and Method Table

Problem Why it is hard Method
Curved walls The true radius and center must be derived from a noisy cloud Several thin sections at different heights, a reference circle on each, averaged over three or four sections
Double-skin glass Reflective glass gives a noisy or missing scan Outer face from the cloud, thickness and gap from drawings and manual measurement, custom curtain wall type with a second curtain wall nested in the panel family at a fixed offset
Heavy file Large footprint and complex geometry Point cloud and linked objects in a separate workset, closed when not in use
Quality control Hundreds of curved walls Heat map against the point cloud, review limited to red areas

Procedure for the Curved Walls

  1. Cut a thin horizontal section through the point cloud.
  2. Draw a reference circle that fits the points on that section.
  3. Repeat at different heights, three or four sections in total.
  4. Compare the center and radius across the sections.
  5. Take the average center line and radius, which filters out scan noise.
  6. Build each wall as one continuous curve, not a chain of short segments.

Quality Control Result

Using the heat map so that reviewers only work the red areas cut quality-control time by roughly 80 to 90 percent, as stated in the project write-up.

Deliverable Use

The model carries LOD information from the supplied technical drawings on each object. That supports renovation, repair, asset management and a feed into a facility management system.
